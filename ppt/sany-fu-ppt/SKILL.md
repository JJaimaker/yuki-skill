# SANY President Fu PPT

Turn raw business material into a page that President Fu can scan for conclusion, evidence, exception, milestone, and management action.

## Reading order

Build the page in this order:

1. Judgment title that names the subject and the main conclusion.
2. One to three top summary lines, each starting with a short bold label. When applicable, include the key number, comparison, and directional judgment.
3. Structured body regions that follow the business's natural lines, such as projects, assistants, markets, or workstreams.
4. Numbered conclusions inside each region, followed by only the evidence needed to support them.
5. Final visible milestone, next action, coordination request, or decision.

Do not copy the source table or feature list directly. First extract what changed, what is proven, what is abnormal, and what needs action.

## Data, comparison, and direction

For progress, operating, customer-value, efficiency, or outcome reporting, do not stop at absolute values. Build the conclusion as:

`current value -> comparison baseline -> change -> direction -> management implication`

- Show at least one relevant current-period number when the source provides it.
- Prefer month-over-month comparison. Use week-over-week, year-over-year, plan-versus-actual, target gap, or before-versus-after only when that is the valid business baseline. Always name the comparison period and denominator.
- State both the absolute change and percentage change when they materially clarify the result.
- Convert the comparison into a directional conclusion such as `持续改善`, `增速放缓`, `风险扩大`, `基本稳定`, or `尚未形成趋势`, then state the business implication or required action.
- Never invent a prior-period value or infer a trend from one observation. If the source lacks a comparable baseline, mark `缺少上期同口径数据，暂不判断环比趋势` and request the missing field.
- Keep metric definitions consistent across periods. If the sample, scope, metric definition, or denominator changes, explain the comparability limit before stating a trend.

## Color semantics

Use color as meaning, never decoration:

- blue text: completed progress, positive evidence, approved scope, or confirmed customer recognition;
- red text: shortfall, abnormality, missing input, overdue item, material risk, or explicit coordination need;
- bold black text: the judgment, key commitment, decision point, milestone, or action that leadership must retain;
- ordinary black or dark gray: explanation and supporting facts;
- gray: pending, not started, or secondary context.

Do not mark a future plan blue. Do not mark every collaboration item red unless it is actually blocking or threatening delivery.

## Content rules

- Separate completed, current, planned, and expected states. Dates alone do not prove delivery.
- Preserve original dates and label future dates as `计划上线`, `计划完成`, or `待确认`.
- Keep customer quotations authentic and attributed. Treat customer recognition as qualitative evidence unless measured outcomes are supplied.
- For AI work, do not turn an internal test, MVP, code completion, or model result into an accepted launch.
- Express a material problem as `问题 -> 影响 -> 解决动作 -> 需协调事项`. Add owner and deadline when supplied; otherwise mark them `待确认`.
- Surface cross-team dependencies because President Fu cares about who must supply what and which milestone it affects.
- Prefer concrete nouns, numbers, customers, equipment models, milestones, and deliverables over abstract progress language.

## Actual PPT production

When the user requests an editable PowerPoint, use the presentation-production skill and keep text, tables, charts, timelines, and simple evidence structures as native editable objects. Render and inspect the final page before delivery.

Place source notes, fact boundaries, missing fields, and caveats in speaker notes when they are important but would overload the page.

## Quality gate

Before delivery, verify that the page answers:

- What is the conclusion in ten seconds?
- What was completed, and what is only planned?
- What numbers, customer evidence, or deliverables support the conclusion?
- Does each material result show the current number, comparison period, and basis or denominator?
- Does the comparison lead to an explicit direction and management implication?
- If no comparable baseline exists, is the missing baseline visibly acknowledged instead of implying a trend?
- Which exception is red, and what milestone does it affect?
- What action, resource, input, or decision is required next?
- Are the blue, red, and bold-black meanings consistent?
- Can the body be read from meeting-room distance without consulting the source spreadsheet?

If source data is incomplete, continue with a best-effort page and mark only the missing fields that could change the conclusion.
